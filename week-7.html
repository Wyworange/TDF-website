✦ My own interaction: eating fruit

This week we started on our individual interactions. I want to make the elephant eat fruit. At first I planned to use object recognition, but there was no printer nearby at the time, so I started with mouth-opening and left/right-eye wink logic instead.

I planned the interaction with Codex in three steps.

  • Before changing anything, Codex used an Explorer to find my saved scene and custom controls, then waited for my approval, as my workspace instructions require.
  • It found that the elephant moves through an “Elephant Travel” parent at a fixed height, within a 15-unit travel radius.
  • Rear up: when my mouth opens, the elephant raises its front legs and balances on its hind feet.
  • Pick fruit: when an apple or banana card is detected, it reaches its trunk toward the matching fruit.
  • The rig already has the bones needed, but there is no ready-made rear-up animation.
  • No extra tree: the apple and banana go on an existing tree mid-way along the course.
  • Winks replace the cards: right eye picks the apple, left eye picks the banana, and blinking with both eyes is ignored.
  • No pre-training is needed. The pre-trained MediaPipe Face Landmarker detects the mouth and eyes; only the trigger thresholds need calibrating on site.
  • Next, I chose where to place the feeding bush.
  • To describe the position more precisely, I drew a circle on the top-down view and showed it to Codex, instead of explaining it in words.
  • A new FRUIT panel sets the rule: right wink = apple, left wink = banana, both eyes ignored.
  • Debug buttons let me test each step without the camera: rear onto hind feet, pick apple or banana, lower, reset the fruit, and move to the feeding bush.
  • Start face camera (one person) switches to the real face input.
  • This checks the mouth-open logic with the real face camera.
  • When I open my mouth, the elephant rises onto its hind feet, and the panel shows “Rising onto hind feet; movement paused”.

Testing the full sequence showed that the elephant has to stand in a fairly precise spot to reach the fruit. That is hard to judge accurately when controlling it for real.

So I added a portal, which lines the elephant up precisely.

  • Claude customized the eye range to fit my own eyes. The readout shows the live mouth value and each eye's closure (L and R).
  • But I didn't feel safe doing it this way, because I felt I wasn't the one in control.
  • Still, it taught me the principle behind it. So when my teammate Chunzi ran into a similar problem, I had her do the adjusting in MediaPipe herself, then export the trained data to Codex.
  • The panel reads “Fruit attached: lifting”.
  • The tuned rule counts a wink when one eye's closure is at least 0.60 and the other's is at most 0.55.

✦ Integration test

Aurora and I finished our two basic interactions first: eating fruit (mine) and picking up the log (hers). Then we tested integrating them on my computer. Even with only two people, the recognition was clearly unstable.

So we tried sitting apart first, keeping our bodies from overlapping as much as possible.

This time, all three of us combined. Three people turned out to be even more unstable, even though we weren't overlapping.

  • Claude suggested splitting the screen, giving each person their own zone in the camera view.
  • Sure enough, it became much more stable: all three performers show as visible, with Aurora on hands, me on face, and Chunzi on upper body.

✦ A final interaction with the log

  • Aurora's hand gestures can both grab and release the log, but our earlier interactions mainly showed grabbing.
  • So we planned for the elephant to put the log down when it reaches the finish, then have one last interaction with it: tired from walking, it sits on the log to rest.
