Butterfly Dome

Concept & Context

Yuwen Wang

A kinetic sculpture that wakes up to a hand gesture: close your hand, open it, and a wave of wings runs over the dome.

✦ Concept

Seven translucent, lace-like butterflies sit on a 3D-printed dome. A webcam watches for one gesture, a fist and then an open palm. When it sees it, the butterflies close and reopen their wings one after another.

✦ Goals

  • One hidden motor drives every butterfly. All the mechanics stay inside the plinth.
  • Delicate wings that read as lace but still survive repeated motion.
  • No touch: a natural hand gesture is the only input.

✦ Context

Made for the Expressive Mechanics project in TDF, where the brief was to make motion itself expressive. Inspired by Iris van Herpen's kinetic, lace-like butterfly pieces and by images of hands releasing butterflies. Opening a hand became the trigger.

Process & Iteration

Yuwen Wang

Five rounds: test in paper, learn the laser cutter, build a mechanism that failed, then replace it with a simpler one that worked.

01Idea works

Paper prototype

Before using any machine, I tested folding wings in paper. One pulled thread can close a pair of wings.

02Rule: webs min. 2 mm

Laser-cut wings

The first cut melted the film (top). At −46% vector power the edge came out clean (bottom). Thin webs tore, so every web is now at least 2 mm.

03Dropped

Pinch-fold wings

Acrylic levers pinched the wings shut. The levers didn't fit as drawn, and closing needed more force than the fishing line could hold.

04Small force, one motor

Split wings + cam drum

Each wing now hinges on tape and is pulled down by a Y-thread. A 6.3 mm pull closes it about 60°. One servo turns a drum of 12 cams, and each cam pulls one line through a PTFE sheath at its own moment.

05Rule: tie with slack

Print & assemble

Dome, plinth and drum printed in PLA. The first lines were too tight, so the wings couldn't close. Lines are now tied at servo 0° with ~0.5 mm slack.

Before: pinch-fold wings. High force, fiddly levers, and the line wasn't strong enough.

→

After: split wings on a tape hinge, a few millimetres of pull, and one servo that times seven butterflies.

Final Prototype & Outcome

Yuwen Wang

The finished dome, with the gesture page running behind it
Ø150 mm dome · polyester wings · PLA

✦ How you use it

✦ How it works

Webcam → MediaPipe hand tracking → Arduino over USB serial → MG90S servo sweeps 0→180→0° → 12-disc cam drum → rockers pull lines in PTFE sheaths → wings close ~60°, one after another.

7butterflies
1servo for all
12timed cams
~9 sper wave

✦ Outcome

  • One gesture plays a full, travelling wave of wings.
  • All the motion comes from one hidden servo.
  • No springs: wings reopen by their own weight.

✦ Next iteration

  • Channels sized to bought parts were tiny and hard to assemble.
  • Not every butterfly got the mechanism.
  • v2: one spool replaces cams and rockers.
