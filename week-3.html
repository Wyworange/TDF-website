✦ Letter writing app

Random attempt:

Initially, I simply told AI Studio to create a letter writing app by using the prompt “Build a letter writing app”.

Overall, it is a very simple and clearly presented letter-writing app, but it feels too generic to create much meaning for users. I think this is partly because an LLM works as a probability predictor. When I simply prompted it to “build a letter-writing app,” it tended to generate the most common and expected interpretation of what a “letter writer” should be, rather than something more distinctive or meaningful.

Design inspiration

My design inspiration came from a post by a girl who had bought a vintage postcard. She used ChatGPT to translate the text on it and was surprised to discover the term “romantic friendship,” a common expression at the time. She also learned that some romantic relationships were described or concealed under the label of “romantic friendship.”

This inspired me to think about whether a letter-writing application could offer more than just writing assistance. Could it also allow users to immerse themselves in someone else’s perspective and, through the letters, learn about the knowledge, social norms, and historical culture of a particular period?

Reflection: This was the original interface generated by AI Studio based on my prompt. Since I did not provide any direct visual references, the interface ended up containing too much text, especially explanations of the underlying knowledge logic about the writer and receiver.

Iteration 1:

I tried to let AI Studio visualize the majority of the text, but I failed.

Images generated by ChatGPT

So I decided to let ChatGPT generate the initial design first, and then use text annotations to explain the interactions within each interface.

Iteration 2:

Taking the generated images as references, I annotated and explained the interaction to AI Studio, and emphasized that it should not add any extra elements that were not included in the reference image.

Reflection: It may have been because my description was not specific enough and I provided too few references.

Iteration 3:

After clarifying the interaction flow, I realized the experience still wasn’t immersive enough. The options for the elements and the writer were still too long and text-heavy. I then tried asking AI to visualize them, but the result was not what I expected — it did not really simplify the text, and the content on the interface felt scattered and poorly aligned. Based on this, I moved on to the next round of adjustments.

Details iteration:

In order to make the interaction more immersive, I removed the relationship-map background and highlighted the people who were most closely connected to the writer.

Reflection: So I made my intentions more specific in the next iteration.

Reflection on future vibe coding:

What I learned from this process is that we need to take a more intentional, designer-led approach when working with AI coding tools. Before moving into development, we should first map out the system architecture, user flow, and each key interaction point. We can then provide the AI with this structure, along with relevant references and our own design specifications. This would make the development process more straightforward and produce results that better reflect our intentions. Ultimately, I see AI-assisted web coding as an implementation tool that helps designers bring their ideas to life, rather than a substitute for the design process itself.
